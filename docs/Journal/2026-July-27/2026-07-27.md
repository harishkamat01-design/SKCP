---

## Relational Database

Learned why PostgreSQL is called a Relational Database Management System (RDBMS).

A relational database stores information in tables that are connected through relationships.

Business relationships naturally become database relationships.

---

## One-to-Many Relationships

Studied the most common database relationship using SKCP examples.

Examples:

- One Customer → Many Orders
- One Supplier → Many Raw Material Deliveries
- One Machine → Many Production Batches
- One Labour → Many Attendance Records

Discovered that most business processes naturally follow One-to-Many relationships.

---

## Many-to-Many Relationships

Learned one of the most important concepts in database design.

Business example:

One Order contains many Products.

One Product can appear in many Orders.

This creates a Many-to-Many relationship.

---

## Junction (Bridge) Table

Discovered why relational databases cannot directly store Many-to-Many relationships.

Introduced the Order Items table as the bridge between Orders and Products.

Architecture:

Orders

↓

Order Items

↓

Products

---

## Ownership of Data

One of today's biggest architectural learnings.

Instead of asking:

"Where can I store this?"

The correct question became:

"Which business object owns this information?"

Examples:

- Product owns Product Details.
- Order owns Customer Information.
- Order Item owns Quantity, Price and Discount.

# Biggest Learnings

Today's biggest realization was that relational databases mirror real business relationships.

I learned that Quantity does not belong to the Product or the Order individually.

It belongs to the relationship between an Order and a Product, which is represented by the Order Items table.

This changed the way I think about database design.
