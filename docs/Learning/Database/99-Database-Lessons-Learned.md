

Today's biggest realization was that a database is not a collection of tables.

It is a collection of business relationships.

The most important learning was understanding that Quantity belongs to the relationship between an Order and a Product rather than to either entity individually.

This changed my thinking from:

"Where can I store this?"

to

"Which business object owns this information?"

This mindset will guide all future database design decisions.


This should become the equivalent of Business Lessons Learned.
Lessons should include:
Database represents business, not software.
Tables come from Business Objects.
Every business event becomes a transaction.
Data Ownership is more important than memorizing Normal Forms.
Normalization mirrors business responsibilities.
Master Data and Transaction Data are different.
Relationships tell the business story.
Foreign Keys preserve business integrity.
Calculated values should not be stored.
Good database design makes Backend development easier.
Database design is business analysis in technical form.
AI depends on a well-designed database.
